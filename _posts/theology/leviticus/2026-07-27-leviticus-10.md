A Collapse of Order

<br>

While [Leviticus 9]({{ '/blog/leviticus/9' | relative_url }}) shows the people correctly offering a sacrifice to the Lord, this chapter shows what happens if an offering is made that is not according to the Lord's commandments.

> Leviticus 10:1 - "And Nadab and Abihu, the sons of Aaron, took either of them his censer, and put fire therein, and put incense thereon, and offered strange fire before the Lord, **which he commanded them not.**"

A key phrase we have noticed throughout the book of Leviticus is the phrase: "as the Lord commanded." Here Nadab and Abihu offered strange fire before the Lord, something that was not commanded.

> Leviticus 10:2 - "And there went out fire from the Lord, and devoured them, and they died before the Lord."

This seems like a harsh punishment. After all, Nadab and Abihu were still making an offering to the Lord, and not to any other god.

> Leviticus 10:3 - "Then Moses said unto Aaron, This is it that the Lord spake, saying, I will be sanctified in them that come nigh me, and before all the people I will be glorified. And Aaron held his peace."

How did Aaron hold his peace, and not say anything, after his sons were just devoured by fire from the Lord? It is certainly possible that he was scared that he would also be devoured by the same fire.

We have previously only seen the Lord communicate to the people through Moses, who acts as his mouthpiece. But here in verse 8 we see the Lord speak directly to Aaron for the first time! 

> Leviticus 10:8 - "And the Lord spake unto Aaron, saying,"

This direct communication by the Lord could honor and reassure Aaron in his silent grief after the death of two of his sons.

What the Lord tells Aaron to do is to "put difference between holy and unholy, and between unclean and clean" (10). This is the first time that this phrase appears in Leviticus, but this does directly mirror God's act of separation during the creation of the world in [Genesis 1]({{ '/blog/genesis/1' | relative_url }}).

In Genesis 1, God creates order out of chaos by separating elements. He divides light from darkness (Genesis 1:4), the waters above from the waters below (Genesis 1:7), and day from night (Genesis 1:14).

The Hebrew word used for "put difference" in Leviticus 10:10 is *badal*. This is the same exact verb used throughout the Genesis creation account for God's acts of dividing.

By separating the holy from the common and the clean from the unclean, the priests act as "sub-creators." Their daily rituals preserve the life-giving order of creation against the encroaching chaos of death and sin.

> Genesis 2:5 - "And the Lord God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it."

Just as Adam was placed by the Lord God in the Garden to work it and keep it, the priests are placed by the Lord in the Tabernacle to maintain boundaries. To blur the line between the holy and the common - as Nadab and Abihu did - invites a collapse of order, and brought immediate judgement.

This is exactly what happens next in Leviticus 10. Moses tells the priests:
* Eat the meal offering without leaven, for it is most holy (12)
* Eat the meal offering in the holy place (13)

In verses 16-18, Moses was angry with Eleazar and Ithamar, the sons of Aaron who were left alive, because they did not eat the sin offering in the holy place.

> Leviticus 10:19 - "And Aaron said unto Moses, Behold, this day have they offered their sin offering and their burnt offering before the Lord; and such things have befallen me: and if I had eaten the sin offering to day, should it have been accepted in the sight of the Lord?"

Aaron pointed out that they had already performed the required offerings, and they did not intend to disregard God's laws. Eating the sin offering was a holy duty meant to symbolize the removal of the people's guilt. Aaron reasons that consuming a holy offering while in his state of mourning would be irreverent and not accepted by the Lord.

> Leviticus 10:20 - "And when Moses heard that, he was content."
