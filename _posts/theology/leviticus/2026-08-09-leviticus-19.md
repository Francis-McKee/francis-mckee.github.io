The Holiness Code

<br>

Modern Bible scholars and rabbis agree that chapter 19 of Leviticus is the key to understanding the covenant of ancient Israel. Many modern scholars believe that one priest composed chapters 17 to 26 of Leviticus. They call these chapters "the Holiness Code", and call the priest the H source.

Bible scholars generally agree that the Holiness Code was completed before the destruction of the First Temple in 586 BC. This means that when the Holiness Code was written, the Israelites were no longer in the desert, but were already living in the land of Israel.

> Leviticus 19:2 - "...Ye shall be holy: for I the Lord your God am holy."

This is the message of the Holiness Code and the heart of the Torah.

The Holiness Code starts by repeating all of the Ten Commandments (see [Exodus 20]({{ '/blog/exodus/20' | relative_url }}), [Exodus 34]({{ '/blog/exodus/34' | relative_url }})), putting them in a new order and sometimes in new words. It places them beside laws that speak about farming, sacrifice, rituals, and the way we should treat strangers, neighbors, and family. The Holiness Code speaks about things everyone can understand: everyday life.

There are a few laws in this chapter that I'd like to highlight:

**Care for the Poor:**

> Leviticus 19:9-10 - "And when ye reap the harvest of your land, thou shalt not wholly reap the corners of thy field, neither shalt thou gather the gleanings of thy harvest. And thou shalt not glean thy vineyard, neither shalt thou gather every grape of thy vineyard; thou shalt leave them for the poor and stranger: I am the Lord your God."

This is not charity, the giving of a willful heart, to the poor or outsider. Rather, the Lord is saying that the poor and outsider have a right to be taken care of by the community (it is a command).

**Fair Legal Decisions:**

> Leviticus 19:15 - "Ye shall do no unrighteousness in judgment: thou shalt not respect the person of the poor, nor honor the person of the mighty: but in righteousness shalt thou judge thy neighbour."

The Holiness Code reminds judges that the rich and the poor are the same, and all decisions must be fair.

**The Golden Rule:**

> Leviticus 19:18 - "Thou shalt not avenge, nor bear any grudge against the children of thy people, but thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself: I am the Lord."

This is what is called the "Golden Rule": to love your neighbor as yourself. When Hillel was asked to sum up everything in the Torah in one rule, he said, "What is hateful to you, do not do to your neighbor." Rabbi Akiba stated that "Love your neighbor as yourself" is "the great principle of the Torah."

> Galatians 5:13-14 - "For, brethren, ye have been called unto liberty; only use not liberty for an occasion to the flesh, but by love serve one another. For all the law is fulfilled in one word, even in this; Thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself."

The Golden Rule here would not be complete without its second half: "I am the Lord." What makes loving our neighbors a holy way to behave is knowing that the Lord loves all human beings equally. We become more like the Lord when we believe and act with the understanding that all humans have value, as they are created in the image of God.
