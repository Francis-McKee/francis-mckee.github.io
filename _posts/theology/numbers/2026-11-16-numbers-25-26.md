A Covenant of Peace

<br>

## Numbers 25

> Numbers 25:1-3 (NASB) - "While Israel remained at Shittim, the people began to commit infidelity with the daughters of Moab. For they invited the people to the sacrifices of their gods, and the people ate and bowed down to their gods. So Israel became followers of Baal of Peor [or, Baal-peor], and the Lord was angry with Israel."

The Lord punishes them with death, saying to Moses: "Take all the leaders of the people and execute them in broad daylight before the Lord, so that the fierce anger of the Lord may turn away from Israel" (4). The Lord also sent a plague onto the people.

Then one of the Israelites came with a Midianite woman into the sight of Moses and the whole congregation. 

> Numbers 25:7-8 (NASB) - "When Phinehas [some translations call him Pinchas] the son of Eleazar, the son of Aaron the priest, saw it, he rose up from the midst of the congregation and took a spear in his hand, and he went after the man of Israel into the inner room of the tent and pierced both of them, the man of Israel and the woman, through the abdomen."

So the Lord brought the plague to a halt, but the total number of people who died from it was 24,000.

> Numbers 25:10-13 (NASB) - "Then the Lord spoke to Moses, saying, "Phinehas the son of Eleazar, the son of Aaron the priest, has averted My wrath from the sons of Israel in that he was jealous with My jealousy among them, so that I did not destroy the sons of Israel in My jealousy. Therefore say, 'Behold, I am giving him My covenant of peace; and it shall be for him and for his descendants after him, a covenant of a permanent priesthood, because he was jealous for his God and made atonement for the sons of Israel.'""

Here the Lord tells Moses that Phinehas has done a good thing. If Phinehas had not killed the man and woman, this plague may have wiped out all the Israelites. The Lord rewards Phinehas with a special "covenant of peace", promising that Phinehas and his descendants will always be priests.

We are then told of the names of the Israelite man (Zimri) and Midianite woman (Cozbi) who were killed by Phinehas. Zimri's father was a chieftain (leader) in the tribe of Simeon. Cozbi's father was a chieftain of the Midianites. As a couple, their influence and worship of the Midianite god Baal-peor caused many Israelites to follow their lead. This is why the Lord ended the plague when this couple were killed.

> Numbers 25:16-18 (NASB) - "Then the Lord spoke to Moses, saying, 'Be hostile to the Midianites and attack them; for they have been hostile to you with their tricks, with which they have deceived you in the matter of Peor and in the matter of Cozbi, the daughter of the leader of Midian, their sister who was killed on the day of the plague because of Peor.'"

## Numbers 26

When the plague ended, the Lord commanded Moses and Eleazar the High Priest to take a new census. The Lord tells Moses that the purpose of the census is to count how many Israelites can be ready to go to war. The tribe of Levi is counted separately, as it was in the last census (see [Numbers 1]({{ '/blog/numbers/1-2' | relative_url }})). Including Levi, thirteen tribes are listed in the census. But the tribes of Ephraim and Manasseh are counted as one, since both were children of Joseph.

In the last census, the total number of Israelite males (minus the Levites) aged 20+ and ready to go to war came to 603,550; this time the total number is 601,730. In the last census, the tribe of Levi totaled 22,000; this time there are 23,000 Levites. 

In the last census, each individual was counted. This time the census counts clans (family-houses) in the tribes. The name of each tribe is given, then its clans are named, then the total is presented. The list looks like this:

> Numbers 26:5-7 (NASB) - "Reuben, Israel’s firstborn, the sons of Reuben: of Hanoch, the family of the Hanochites; of Pallu, the family of the Palluites; of Hezron, the family of the Hezronites; of Carmi, the family of the Carmites. These are the families of the Reubenites, and those who were counted of them were 43,730."

In the tribe of Manasseh, when it comes to the clan of Zelophedad, the list changes. The text says:

> Numbers 26:33 (NASB) - "Now Zelophehad the son of Hepher had no sons, only daughters; and the names of the daughters of Zelophehad were Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah."

We will see more about them in the next chapter.

The count ended, and the Lord gave Moses another reason for the census. Even though the Israelites had not yet conquered Canaan, they were to divide the land according to their clans. The Lord commanded:

> Numbers 26:54-56 (NASB) - "To a larger group you shall increase their inheritance, and to a smaller group you shall decrease their inheritance; each shall be given their inheritance corresponding to the total of those who were numbered of them. But the land shall be divided by lot. They shall receive their inheritance according to the names of the tribes of their fathers. Corresponding to the selection by lot, their inheritance shall be divided between the larger and the smaller groups."

Dividing Canaan would not be an easy task. If larger shares had to go to larger clans, the Israelites could work that out. By why does the Lord then say that the land should be divided by casting lots (meaning God would control the outcome)?

The Bible commentator Abrabanel (1437 - 1508) proposed a solution: Lots were first thrown to see which tribe received which part of Canaan. Then the Israelites gave more usable land to the larger tribes and less to the smaller tribes. Afterward, lots were thrown to divide the tribal lands by clan, and it was up to each tribe to be sure to give more usable land to larger clans, and less to smaller clans. In other words, the Israelites made sure that the size of the portions was fair, but the lots decided the actual place of each tribe and clan. That way the work of dividing was shared by God and the Israelites.
