A Talking Donkey

<br>

When Balak, the new king of Moab, saw how Israel defeated the Amorites (see [Numbers 21]({{ '/blog/numbers/21c' | relative_url }})), he asked his neighbors, the Midianites, to fight with him. The language the text uses at this point is the same as the reaction of the Egyptians at the beginning of the book of Exodus:

> Exodus 1:9,12 (NASB) - "And he [Pharaoh] said to his people, 'Behold, the people of the sons of Israel are too many [*rab*] and too mighty for us...' so that they [the Egyptians] dreaded [*quts*] the sons of Israel."

> Numbers 22:3 (NASB) - "So Moab was in great fear because of the people, for they were numerous [*rab*]; and Moab was in dread [*quts*] of the sons of Israel."

Balak sent messengers to Balaam (a non-Israelite) saying: 

> Numbers 22:5-6 (NASB) - "...'Behold, a people came out of Egypt; behold, they have covered the surface of the land, and they are living opposite me. Now, therefore, please come, curse this people for me since they are too mighty for me; perhaps I will be able to defeat them and drive them out of the land. For I know that he whom you bless is blessed, and he whom you curse is cursed.'"

Balaam said to the messengers, "Spend the night here, and I will bring word back to you just as the Lord may speak to me" (8). That night the Lord came to Balaam and told him, "Do not go with them; you shall not curse the people, for they are blessed" (12). Balaam arose the next morning and sent the messengers away. Then Balak sent high officials as messengers to plead with Balaam. "I beg you, let nothing keep you from coming to me; for I will indeed honor you richly" (16-17). But Balaam replied, saying that nothing Balak promises to give him is enough, if the Lord does not want him to go (18). But again Balaam asked the messengers to stay overnight. That night, God told Balaam, "...rise and go with them; but you shall do only the thing that I tell you" (20).

## Who is Balaam?

Balak sent two sets of messengers to Balaam. Both times Balaam asked them to wait overnight for their answer. Both times, the Lord came to Balaam to give him an answer. In [Numbers 12], the Lord spoke to Miriam and Aaron, saying:

> NUmbers 12:6 (NASB) - "...'Now hear My words: If there is a prophet among you, I, the Lord, will make Myself known to him in a vision. I will speak with him in a dream.'"

The text does not call Balaam a prophet at the start, but every Jew hearing this story would know that when Balaam hears the Lord answer in the night, this means that Balaam is a prophet.

In 1967, archeologists discovered an inscription at Deir Alla, which is located in western Jordan. With this discovery we learned that Balaam was well known, even famous, in the days of early Israel. In the stele, Balaam is called a "seer of the gods" (remember, Balaam is not an Israelite). See References below for a link to Wikipedia on the Deir Alla inscription.

It was customary among pre-Islamic Arabs to hire people thought to be under divine influence to compose curses against their enemies - essentially Balaam was a prophet for hire, who did not have allegiance to any particular people:

> Numbers 22:6 (NASB) - "...For I know that he whom you bless is blessed, and he whom you curse is cursed."

## A Talking Donkey

Stories like this, featuring talking animals, have been known in fairy tales and folklore forever. This story, like the one in [Genesis 3]({{ '/blog/genesis/2-3' | relative_url }}) about the talking snake, was probably told by the J-teller.

The following morning Balaam arose, saddled his donkey, and went with the officials. The text says God was very angry and sent the angel of the Lord to stop Balaam. When Balaam's donkey saw the angel of the Lord, it left the road and went into the fields. Balaam beat the donkey to force her to return to the road. 

> Numbers 22:24-27 (NASB) - "Then the angel of the Lord stood in a narrow path of the vineyards, with a stone wall on this side and on that side. When the donkey saw the angel of the Lord, she pressed herself against the wall and pressed Balaam’s foot against the wall, so he struck her again. Then the angel of the Lord went farther, and stood in a narrow place where there was no way to turn to the right or to the left. When the donkey saw the angel of the Lord, she lay down under Balaam; so Balaam was angry and struck the donkey with his staff."

The Lord opened the mouth of the donkey. She said to Balaam, "What have I done to you, that you have struck me these three times?" Balaam responded, "It is because you have made a mockery of me! If only there had been a sword in my hand! For I would have killed you by now!" But the donkey said to Balaam, "Am I not your donkey on which you have ridden all your life to this day? Have I ever been in the habit of doing such a thing to you?" And Balaam said, "No." (28-30)

> Numbers 22:31-33 (NASB) - "Then the Lord opened Balaam’s eyes, and he saw the angel of the Lord standing in the way with his sword drawn in his hand; and he bowed all the way to the ground. Then the angel of the Lord said to him, 'Why have you struck your donkey these three times? Behold, I have come out as an adversary, because your way was reckless and contrary to me. But the donkey saw me and turned away from me these three times. If she had not turned away from me, I certainly would have killed you just now, and let her live.'"

> Numbers 22:34-35 (NASB) - "So Balaam said to the angel of the Lord, 'I have sinned, for I did not know that you were standing in the way against me. Now then, if it is displeasing to you, I will turn back.' But the angel of the Lord said to Balaam, 'Go with the men, but you shall speak only the word that I tell you.' So Balaam went along with the representatives of Balak."

King Balak came out to meet Balaam, asking why Balaam didn't come when he was first invited. Balaam answered, "Behold, I have come to you now! Am I really able to speak anything? The word that God puts in my mouth, that only shall I speak" (38). That night Balak made a feast for Balaam and the next morning he took Balaam up on a hill. From there, they could see a part of the Israelite camp.

## Problems

What about the talking donkey? And why does the text say that God was angry with Balaam for going to see Balak?

> Numbers 22:20 (NASB) - "And God came to Balaam at night and said to him, 'If the men have come to call you, rise and go with them; but you shall do only the thing that I tell you.'"

> Numbers 22:22 (NASB) - "But God was angry that he was going..."

Had God changed His mind?

Balaam had asked God once (10-11), and God said "no" (12). That should have sufficed, yet Balaam asked a second time (19). He knew already that God did not want him to go. Yet he invited the second set of messengers to wait overnight in case God had changed His mind. 

Balaam did not accept God's first refusal. He wanted to hear the answer "yes". And that is indeed what he heard the second time he asked, not because God wanted him to go, but because God does not force His will upon us. The true meaning of God's second reply, "go with them", is, "if you insist, then I can not stop you going - but I am angry that you should have asked a second time." 

God did not change His ming at any point in the narrative. God did not want Balaam to go, but Balaam was not willing to hear and accept God's no. When God speaks and we do not listen, He does not intervene to save us from our choices. 

God then proceeds to show Balaam that when He so chooses, even Balaam's donkey is a greater prophet than he. The donkey sees what Balaam can not see: the angel in the path, barring the way.
