Purity

<br>

If an Israelite becomes impure - by a disease, unusual bleeding, fluid release, or contact with a corpse - that person must be sent outside of the camp. (2-3)

> Numbers 5:4 - "And the children of Israel did so, and put them out without the camp: as the Lord spake unto Moses, so did the children of Israel."

It was dangerous to have an impure person inside of the camp because it was thought that others became inpure simply by touching someone who was impure. If people in the camp become impure, then eventually, the entire camp could become impure, including the Levite priests.

The laws in this chapter are about keeping the camp pure - not just physically pure, but also pure in spirit. We see this in the next law of the chapter:

> Numbers 5:6-7 - "6 Speak unto the children of Israel, When a man or woman shall commit any sin that men commit, to do a trespass against the Lord, and that person be guilty; Then they shall confess their sin which they have done: and he shall recompense his trespass with the principal thereof, and add unto it the fifth part thereof, and give it unto him against whom he hath trespassed."

This law looks familiar - in fact it is a repeat of the trespass offering for intentional sin, found in [Leviticus 6]({{ '/blog/leviticus/6' | relative_url }}). There are a few key details added here:

* The Kinsman-Redeemer: specifies what happens if the person who was wronged has died and has no close relative to receive the payment. The restitution goes to the priest instead. (8)
* The Priestly Share: clarifies the logistics of the accompanying ram of atonement and holy gifts as an extra benefit for the officiating priests (8-10)

## The "Ordeal"

This chapter also concerns the purity of marriage. The family is the primary building block of Israelite society. Like the covenant with the Lord, the covenant between husband and wife is sacred and must not be broken. If it is broken, it brings impurity to the camp.

If a husband suspects that his wife has broken their marriage vows, he may bring his wife (and a grain offering) to the priest. The priest tests the wife to see if she was unfaithful. A ritual test like this is called an "ordeal". The specific ordeal used here by the priest is called *sotah*, meaning "gone astray".

How can we know what a person has done in secret? If there is no witness, how can we tell if a person is guilty of a crime? The ordeal declares that there is always one witness, even when no one seems to be present, and that witness is God. Through the ordeal, God is "called" to the witness stand to declare a person guilty or not guilty.

We have seen this ordeal used already, back in [Exodus 32]({{ '/blog/exodus/32' | relative_url }}) after the sin of the golden calf! I won't repeat all of the ordeal here, but you can read the specific steps in verses 11-31.
