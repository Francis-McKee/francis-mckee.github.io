Israelite Conquests

<br>

Now the Israelites moved from camp to camp. The text quotes from a scroll called "The Book of the Wars of the Lord", telling the exact boundary between the land of Moab and the land of the Amorites. After a while they arrived at Beer (meaning "a well"), where the Lord told Moses, "Assemble the people, that I may give them water" (16). Then the Israelites sang:

> Numbers 21:18 (NASB) - "'Spring up, O well! Sing to it! The well, which the leaders dug,
Which the nobles of the people hollowed out,
With the scepter and with their staffs.'"

This is called "the Song at the Well". Some scholars think it also may be quoted from "The Book of the Wars of the Lord". No copy of this scroll seems to have survived; all we have are the small parts thar are quoted here. It was either a history of conquests or a book of songs praising the Lord in times of war. Bible scholars see this as additional evidence that the Torah was made up of earlier scrolls that were later combined.

Leaving Beer, the Israelites came near a mountain peak called Pisgah. Here messengers were sent to King Sihon of the Amorites, repeating the message Moses sent to the Edomites in [Numbers 20]({{ '/blog/numbers/20c-21a' | relative_url }}):

> Numbers 21:22 (NASB) - "Let me pass through your land. We will not turn off into field or vineyard; we will not drink water from wells. We will go by the king’s road until we have passed through your border."

But King Sihon refused to let them pass. Instead, he marched his army out and attacked the Israelites. This time the Israelites fought, defeating King Sihon and capturing the Amorite towns, including Heshbon, their capital.

Hesbon had once belonged to King Ar of Moab. Some time before, King Sihon and his armies had captured it from the Moabites, and the king's poet-singers celebrated this victory with a popular song:

> Numbers 21:28-29 (NASB) - "Come to Heshbon! Let it be built! So let the city of Sihon be established. For a fire spread from Heshbon,
A flame from the town of Sihon; It devoured Ar of Moab, The dominant heights of the Arnon. Woe to you, Moab! You are destroyed, people of Chemosh! He has given his sons as fugitives, And his daughters into captivity, To an Amorite king, Sihon."

The Israelites celebrated their victory over Heshbon by adding a new ending to the song:

> Numbers 21:30 (NASB) - "But we [Israel] have shot them [the Amorites] down with arrows, Heshbon is destroyed as far as Dibon, Then we have laid waste as far as Nophah, Which reaches to Medeba."

After defeating the Amorites, Moses sent spies to the town of Jazer, which they later attacked and conquered, dispelling the Amorites who lived there. They then marched up the road to Bashan, where King Og and his soldiers tried to stop them.

> Numbers 21:34-35 (NASB) - "But the Lord said to Moses, 'Do not fear him, for I have handed him over to you, and all his people and his land; and you shall do to him as you did to Sihon, king of the Amorites, who lived in Heshbon.' So they [the Israelites] killed him [King Og] and his sons and all his people, until there was no survivor left; and they took possession of his land."

## Land as Inheritance

From the story of King Sihon we can see that war and conquest were common even among the Canaanites. Sihon and the Amorites, a stronger army, captured Heshbon from the Moabites and their weaker army. Then the Israelites captured Heshbon from the Amorites. 

When the text speaks of the Israelites conquering lands, it often uses the word *yirash*, "inherited". In other words, the lands belonged to them already because of the covenants the Lord made with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob:

Covenants Promising Land:
* Abraham - [Genesis 12:7]({{ '/blog/genesis/11b-12a' | relative_url }}), [Genesis 13:14-15]({{ '/blog/genesis/13-14' | relative_url }}), [Genesis 15:18-21]({{ '/blog/genesis/15' | relative_url }}), and [Genesis 17:8]({{ '/blog/genesis/17' | relative_url }})
* Isaac - [Genesis 26:3]({{ '/blog/genesis/26'}})
* Jacob - [Genesis 28:13]({{ '/blog/genesis/28'}}) and [Genesis 35:12]({{ '/blog/genesis/34-35'}})

> Genesis 17:8 (NASB) - "And I will give to you [Abraham] and to your descendants after you the land where you live as a stranger, all the land of Canaan, as an everlasting possession; and I will be their God." 

> Genesis 26:3 (NASB) - "Live for a time in this land and I [the Lord] will be with you [Isaac] and bless you, for to you and to your descendants I will give all these lands, and I will establish the oath which I swore to your father Abraham."

> Genesis 35:12 (NASB) - "And the land which I [the Lord] gave to Abraham and Isaac, I will give to you [Jacob], And I will give the land to your descendants after you."

Clearly war and death were necessary in the conquest of land, but the outcome depended on the Lord.
