A "Quiet Revolution"

<br>

This chapter contains instructions to Aaron and his family. This time, the Lord speaks directly to Aaron - Moses is not addressed here, since he is just a Levite, a servant of the High Priests.

> Numbers 18:1-7 (NASB) - "So the Lord said to Aaron, “You, your sons, and your father’s household with you... shall bear the guilt in connection with your priesthood. But also [you shall be aided by] the tribe of Levi... And they shall perform duties for you and the duties of the whole tent, but they shall not come near the furnishings of the sanctuary and the altar, or both they and you will die... I am giving you the priesthood as a service that is a gift, and the unauthorized person who comes near shall be put to death."

Offerings and sacred donations made by the Israelites to the Lord are assigned to Aaron and his sons. The best of everything that is brought to the Lord - the best of the new oil, the wine, the grain, and the meat -  will be the food of the High Priest and his family.

> Numbers 18:19 (NASB) - "All the offerings of the holy gifts, which the sons of Israel offer to the Lord, I have given to you and your sons and your daughters with you, as a permanent allotment. It is a permanent covenant of salt before the Lord to you and your descendants with you."

Throughout history, salt was used in almost everything that was eaten. Salt helped preserve foods. Ancient people spoke of sharing a meal as "taking salt together". Often the Bible speaks of an agreement made through eating together as a "covenant of salt". 

In return, the Lord will give no land to Aaron and the priests. Nor will their servants, the Levites, have any land. The priests and the Levites will take a tithe from the rest of the tribes, a tenth of everything. But the Levites will also give a tithe, a tenth of all they take, as a gift to the Lord. And this gift will then be given to Aaron the High Priest, and assigned to the priests.

This special covenant between the Lord and Aaron was part of a change that placed the priests above the Levites. This "quiet revolution" changed the priesthood of Israel forever, turning one family (Aaronites) into "higher priests" or *kohanim*, and leaving all other priestly families as servants or Levites. The events of [Numbers 16]({{ '/blog/numbers/16' | relative_url }}) and [Numbers 17]({{ '/blog/numbers/17' | relative_url }}) confirmed this "revolution".

Once the Levites served local shrines and altars throughout the land of Israel. But under Solomon, the Temple became the only shrine and the only altar for all Israel; and one family - the *kohanim*, the family that claimed to be descended from Aaron and his sons - became the High Priests for all Israel. 

The Israelites came to have three "levels" or "divisions" of holiness:
1. Aaronite priests (*kohanim*)
2. Levites
3. Israelites

This chapter contains parallelism to illustrate this point, showing who is allowed in versus who is kept out:
* A: Aaronite responsibility (1)
* B: The Levite role (2)
* C: Lethal boundary (3-4)
* D: The Goal (5)
* B': The Levite role (6)
* A': Aaronite responsibility (7a)
* C': Lethal boundary (7b)

Middle:
> Numbers 18:5 - "So you shall perform the duties of the sanctuary and the duties of the altar, so that there will no longer be wrath on the sons of Israel."

The sole reason these levels or divisions of holiness exist between Aaron and his family, the Levites, and the rest of the Israelites is to prevent the deadly wrath of God from breaking out against the community again (like in [Numbers 16]({{ '/blog/numbers/16' | relative_url }})). This preventative purpose is the very heartbeat of biblical atonement: averting judgement by meeting God's holy requirements through divinely appointed mediation.
