Moses Denied Entry

<br>

Why doesn't Moses (and Aaron) get to enter the Promised Land?

> Numbers 20:12 (NASB) - "But the Lord said to Moses and Aaron, 'Since you did not trust in Me, to treat Me as holy in the sight of the sons of Israel, for that reason you shall not bring this assembly into the land which I have given them.'"

Moses has been leading the people for a long time! This is the guy who has put his own neck on the line for the people:

> Exodus 32:31-32 (NASB) - "Then Moses returned to the Lord and said, 'Oh, this people has committed a great sin, and they have made a god of gold for themselves! But now, if You will forgive their sin, very well; but if not, please wipe me out from Your book which You have written!'"

In [Numbers 12]({{ '/blog/numbers/12' | relative_url }}), Moses is even described as the most humble person on the face of the earth:

> Numbers 12:3 (NASB) - "(Now the man Moses was very humble, more than any person who was on the face of the earth.)"

We have seen the character of Moses and his integrity.

We have also seen a God of forgiveness. One example is the story of the Golden Calf in [Exodus 32]({{ '/blog/exodus/32' | relative_url }}). We have seen Him forgive some pretty big mistakes.

Is it because Moses hit the rock instead of speaking to it? 

> Numbers 20:8,11 (NASB) - "'Take the staff; and you and your brother Aaron assemble the congregation and speak to the rock before their eyes, that it shall yield its water...' Then Moses raised his hand and struck the rock twice with his staff; and water came out abundantly, and the congregation and their livestock drank."

Not only did he hit it once, but Moses doubled down and hit the rock twice.

Still, it seems extreme that Moses would be denied entry into the Promised Land simply because he didn't follow instructions.

Is it because Moses took credit for God's actions?

> Numbers 20:10 (NASB) - "and Moses and Aaron summoned the assembly in front of the rock. And he said to them, 'Listen now, you rebels; shall **we** bring water for you out of this rock?'"

Moses acted like it was his power, and not God's. Okay, so Moses got a little full of himself and misspoke. Still, the punishment seems a bit extreme. 

After all, the Lord uses similar language when speaking to Moses:

> Numbers 20:7-8 (NASB) - "then the Lord spoke to Moses, saying, 'Take the staff; and you and your brother Aaron assemble the congregation and speak to the rock before their eyes, that it shall yield its water. So **you** shall bring water for them out of the rock, and have the congregation and their livestock drink.'"

God says "you" (as in Moses) will bring forth the water. We know it's God and His power that brought the water forth. But it seems weird that Moses essentially says what God says to Him, and then he is denied entry into the Promised Land. There must be more to this story.

How many times do the Israelites complain about not having any water to drink? It may seem like they complain about this often, but the Israelites only complain about not having water twice - first in [Exodus 17]({{ '/blog/exodus/17a' | relative_url }}), and then again here in Numbers 20 when Miriam dies. 

Jewish tradition teaches that Miriam was connected with the water, and with its source, a rock (also sometimes called a well). As long as she was alive, there was water. Right after Miriam died, that was when the Israelites complained they had no water to drink. Miriam was connected to the one who brought the water. Rabbis of the Midrash believe the rock (or well) followed the Israelites throughout their journey in the wilderness! That is why they only complained about having no water twice.

> 1 Corinthians 10:1-4 (NASB) - "For I do not want you to be unaware, brothers and sisters, that our fathers were all under the cloud and they all passed through the sea; and they all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea; and they all ate the same spiritual food, and all drank the same spiritual drink, for they were drinking from a spiritual rock **which followed them**; and the rock was Christ."

The writer of 1 Corinthians (Paul) is clearly referencing rabbinic tradition here.

There is another problem with this text.
There in no period of mourning for Miriam here. Later in this chapter, we will read about mourning for the death of Aaron. We will also later see mourning for the death of Moses. Is Moses angry because the people do not mourn his sister? In Exodus 17, Moses struck a rock, and water came out for the people. But Moses and Aaron did not speak out angrily then, as they did here when Moses said, "Listen now, you rebels..." (9).

Also, where does Miriam's death and this event take place? We have two conflicting verses which give us two different names for the place:

> Numbers 20:1 (NASB) - "Then the sons of Israel, the whole congregation, came to the wilderness of Zin in the first month; and the people stayed at Kadesh. Now Miriam died there and was buried there."

> Numbers 20:13 (NASB) - "Those were called the waters of Meribah, because the sons of Israel argued with the Lord, and He proved Himself holy among them."

Is the place called Kadesh, or Meribah? Immediately after verse 13, we read:

> Numbers 20:14 (NASB) - "From Kadesh Moses then sent messengers to the king of Edom..."

If the place is called Kadesh, why does verse 13 say Meribah?

## Solving this Mystery

In order to solve the mystery of this story, we need to look closer at [Exodus 17]({{ '/blog/exodus/17a' | relative_url }}), the only other instance where the Israelites complained about not having water to drink, and where Moses struck the rock to bring forth water for the people.

In Exodus 17, the people were very clearly testing God. In this chapter, the people are testing Moses:

> Numbers 20:3-5 (NASB) - "Then the people argued with Moses and spoke, saying, 'If only we had perished when our brothers perished before the Lord! Why then have you [Moses] brought the Lord’s assembly into this wilderness, for us and our livestock to die here? Why did you [Moses] make us come up from Egypt, to bring us into this wretched place? It is not a place of grain or figs or vines or pomegranates, nor is there water to drink!'"

The name Meribah may look familiar to some people. We have seen this name before - in Exodus 17:

> Exodus 17:7 (NASB) - "Then he named the place Massah and Meribah because of the quarrel of the sons of Israel, and because they tested the Lord, saying, 'Is the Lord among us, or not?'"

These stories are definitely tied together. Let's compare the text of these two stories:

> Exodus 17:5-6 (NASB) - "Then the Lord said to Moses, 'Pass before the people and take with you some of the elders of Israel; and take in your hand your staff with which you struck the Nile, and go. **Behold, I will stand before you there on the rock at Horeb;** and you shall strike the rock, and water will come out of it, so that the people may drink.' And Moses did so in the sight of the elders of Israel."

> Numbers 20:9-11 (NASB) - "So Moses took the staff from before the Lord, just as He had commanded him; **and Moses and Aaron summoned the assembly in front of the rock.** And he said to them, 'Listen now, you rebels; shall we bring water for you out of this rock?' Then Moses raised his hand and struck the rock twice with his staff; and water came out abundantly, and the congregation and their livestock drank."

If we look at Exodus 17, the Lord stands between Moses and the rock, allowing Himself to be struck by Moses. Looking at Numbers 20, we see that Moses and Aaron summon the people ("the assembly") in front of the rock!

"struck" - Strong's Concordance [5221] = *nakah*
* to strike, smite, hit, beat, slay, kill

This same word is used in Numbers 20, Exodus 17, and also in Exodus 2:12 when Moses slew [5221] the Egyptian who was smiting [5221] a Hebrew slave.

Notice that in Numbers 20:8, the Lord tells Moses to speak to the rock. The Lord knows Moses is angry after the death of his sister. He wants Moses to use his voice, not his staff. But Moses' anger gets the best of him, and it is directed at the people!

> Numbers 20:12 (NASB) - "But the Lord said to Moses and Aaron, 'Since you did not trust in Me, to treat Me as holy in the sight of the sons of Israel, for that reason you shall not bring this assembly into the land which I have given them.'"

The Lord is different because He is a God of peace, love, mercy, and forgiveness. Moses had an opportunity to show the people the holy character of the Lord. But because Moses allowed his anger to get the best of him, he failed to show the Lord to be holy. Moses made the Lord look like all the other gods that are out there. In the end, the text says that "He [the Lord] proved Himself holy among them" (13). The problem was not that the Lord's holiness was not proved - it was proved, but by the Lord Himself, not by Moses.

The way of the Lord's Kingdom of Shalom is not the way of Pharaoh's Kingdom of Empire - the methods used are different:
* Empire (Staff) - using violence, authority, or your title/position to force people to do what you want
* Shalom (Voice) - being a person of character and integrity that would invite people and that people would want to follow

We will come to another story soon (in the book of Numbers) that shows this contrast more clearly.
