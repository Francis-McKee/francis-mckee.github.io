Dividing the Land

<br>

## Numbers 26

Immediately after the plague in [Numbers 25]({{ '/blog/numbers/25' | relative_url }}), the Lord commanded Moses and Eleazar the High Priest to take a new census. The Lord tells Moses that the purpose of the census is to count how many Israelites can be ready to go to war. The tribe of Levi is counted separately, as it was in the last census (see [Numbers 1]({{ '/blog/numbers/1-2' | relative_url }})). Including Levi, thirteen tribes are listed in the census. But the tribes of Ephraim and Manasseh are counted as one, since both were children of Joseph.

In the last census, the total number of Israelite males (minus the Levites) aged 20+ and ready to go to war came to 603,550; this time the total number is 601,730. In the last census, the tribe of Levi totaled 22,000; this time there are 23,000 Levites. 

In the last census, each individual was counted. This time the census counts clans (family-houses) in the tribes. The name of each tribe is given, then its clans are named, then the total is presented. The list looks like this:

> Numbers 26:5-7 (NASB) - "Reuben, Israel’s firstborn, the sons of Reuben: of Hanoch, the family of the Hanochites; of Pallu, the family of the Palluites; of Hezron, the family of the Hezronites; of Carmi, the family of the Carmites. These are the families of the Reubenites, and those who were counted of them were 43,730."

In the tribe of Manasseh, when it comes to the clan of Zelophehad, the list changes. The text says:

> Numbers 26:33 (NASB) - "Now Zelophehad the son of Hepher had no sons, only daughters; and the names of the daughters of Zelophehad were Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah."

We will see more about them in the next chapter.

The count ended, and the Lord gave Moses another reason for the census. Even though the Israelites had not yet conquered Canaan, they were to divide the land according to their clans. The Lord commanded:

> Numbers 26:54-56 (NASB) - "To a larger group you shall increase their inheritance, and to a smaller group you shall decrease their inheritance; each shall be given their inheritance corresponding to the total of those who were numbered of them. But the land shall be divided by lot. They shall receive their inheritance according to the names of the tribes of their fathers. Corresponding to the selection by lot, their inheritance shall be divided between the larger and the smaller groups."

Dividing Canaan would not be an easy task. If larger shares had to go to larger clans, the Israelites could work that out. By why does the Lord then say that the land should be divided by casting lots (meaning God would control the outcome)?

The Bible commentator Abrabanel (1437 - 1508) proposed a solution: Lots were first thrown to see which tribe received which part of Canaan. Then the Israelites gave more usable land to the larger tribes and less to the smaller tribes. Afterward, lots were thrown to divide the tribal lands by clan, and it was up to each tribe to be sure to give more usable land to larger clans, and less to smaller clans. In other words, the Israelites made sure that the size of the portions was fair, but the lots decided the actual place of each tribe and clan. That way the work of dividing was shared by God and the Israelites.

## Numbers 27

Before the land could be divided, the daughters of Zelophehad came forward.

> Numbers 27:1-4 (NASB) - "...these are the names of his daughters: Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah. They stood before Moses, before Eleazar the priest, before the leaders, and all the congregation at the entrance of the tent of meeting, saying, 'Our father died in the wilderness, yet he was not among the group of those who gathered together against the Lord, in the group of Korah; but he died in his own sin, and he had no sons. Why should the name of our father be withdrawn from among his family simply because he had no son? Give us property among our father’s brothers.'"

The five daughters bring a court case, a legal question. The daughters demand that they be given land like every other clan, even though there is no man in the family. 

None of the daughters are married - we know this because if any one were married, her share would be with her husband's clan. We also know that their father is dead. He died in the wilderness, and was not a part of the Korah rebellion that banded together against the Lord ([Numbers 16]({{ '/blog/numbers/16' | relative_url }})). Last, the daughters point out, if they do not receive any land, then the clan of Zelophehad ("our father's name") will disappear. If they have land, then when they marry, their husbands can leave their clans and come to the land of Zelophehad, and any children they have will continue the "name" of Zelophehad. 

Archeologists tell us that in other nations - in Mesopotamia to the north and Egypt to the south - women had the right to own and inherit land from their fathers and husbands for more than a thousand years before Moses. But the Lord had not yet given a command to Moses about women and land. Since God was to play a part in dividing the land (by the lots), Moses "brought their case before the Lord" (5).

> Numbers 27:6-7 (NASB) - "Then the Lord said to Moses, 'The daughters of Zelophehad are right about their statements. You shall certainly give them hereditary property among their father’s brothers, and you shall transfer the inheritance of their father to them.'"

If a man dies and has no son, then the inheritance would be transferred to his daughter. If the man has no children, then the inheritance would be given to his brothers. If he has no children and no brothers, then the inheritance would be transferred to his father's brothers. If the man had no children, no brothers, and his father did not have any brothers, then the inheritance would be transferred to the nearest relative in his own family. (8-11)

The text concludes by saying: "...it shall be a statutory ordinance to the sons of Israel, just as the Lord has commanded Moses" (11). Zelophehad's daughters had won their case.
