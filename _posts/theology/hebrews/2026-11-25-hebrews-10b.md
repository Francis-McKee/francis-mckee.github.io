Therefore Persevere

<br>

> Hebrews 10:19-22 (NASB) - "Therefore, brothers and sisters, since we have confidence to enter the holy place by the blood of Jesus, by a new and living way which He inaugurated for us through the veil, that is, through His flesh, and since we have a great priest over the house of God, let’s approach God with a sincere heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled clean from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water."

On the Day of Atonement, the High Priest entered the Most Holy Place with fear and trembling, but we can enter with confidence because we enter by the blood of Jesus. It is not about our perfection or ability to follow the law, it is about Christ. 

Under the Old Covenant, the High Priest had access because of the blood of a dead animal. Now, under the New Covenant, we have access because of the perfect sacrifice of the sinless Son of God who is resurrected and living.

The veil separated the Most Holy Place (also called the "Holy of Holies") from the Holy Place. To enter the Most Holy Place, you had to pass through the veil. But this veil separating man from God is forever opened wide, being torn in two from top to bottom:

> Matthew 27:50-51 (NASB) - "And Jesus cried out again with a loud voice, and gave up His spirit. And behold, the veil of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom; and the earth shook and the rocks were split."

The writer of Hebrews makes an analogy between the veil that stood between God and man and the body of Jesus. Jesus' body was "torn", and so was the veil, each indicating that now we can come to God boldly.

We have a High Priest (Jesus Christ) who presides over the heavenly courts to make sure the believer has total access.

With the perfect cleansing available to us, described in terms of promises of the New Covenant in the Hebrew Scriptures (hearts sprinkled) and the Christian practice of baptism (bodies washed), we can draw near to God in a way never available to someone under the Old Covenant. The work of Jesus makes us able to draw near in a "full assurance of faith". (22)

We can draw near because several issues have been settled. The problem of access to God has been settled. The problem of a perfect High Priest has been settled. The problem of moral and spiritual pollution has been settled.

The encouragement to draw near wouldn't be given unless it was necessary. The audience of the book of Hebrews (discouraged Jewish Christians) had a problem in drawing near. This was their real problem: they lost their intimate relationship with Jesus. They didn't draw near to God on the basis of what Jesus had already done, and nothing else in their lives is going right. Just as importantly, the original readers of this letter are reminded that they will never regain that close relationship with God by coming through the institutions of the Old Covenant.

> Hebrews 10:23-25 (NASB) - "Let’s hold firmly to the confession of our hope without wavering, for He who promised is faithful; and let’s consider how to encourage one another in love and good deeds, not abandoning our own meeting together, as is the habit of some people, but encouraging one another; and all the more as you see the day drawing near."

Discouragement made the Jewish Christians waver from the truth. A renewed confidence in the greatness of Jesus and the New Covenant will make them stand strong in the faith. The reason we can stand strong is because "He who promised is faithful" (23). It is far better to trust in His faithfulness instead of ours!

Discouragement made them avoid community at the very time they needed it most. Jesus meets us in one another to stir up love and good works. Forsaking fellowship is a sure way to give place to discouragement. This discouragement festers where God's people are not exhorting one another. As the Day of Jesus' return draws nearer, we should be more committed to the fellowship of God's people, the assembling of ourselves together.

> Hebrews 10:26-31 (NASB) - "For if we go on sinning willfully after receiving the knowledge of the truth, there no longer remains a sacrifice for sins, but a terrifying expectation of judgment and the fury of a fire which will consume the adversaries. Anyone who has ignored the Law of Moses is put to death without mercy on the testimony of two or three witnesses. How much more severe punishment do you think he will deserve who has trampled underfoot the Son of God, and has regarded as unclean the blood of the covenant by which he was sanctified, and has insulted the Spirit of grace? For we know Him who said, 'Vengeance is Mine, I will repay.' And again, 'The Lord will judge His people.' It is a terrifying thing to fall into the hands of the living God."

To "sin willfully" is defined in verse 29. It speaks of someone who has "trampled underfoot the Son of God", has "regarded as unclean (common) the blood of the covenant by which he was sanctified", and has "insulted the Spirit of grace". It is a knowing, deliberate rejection of Jesus' great work for us on the cross.

In a sense, every sin is a "willfull sin". But here, the writer to the Hebrews spoke of something much more sever and relevant to these discouraged Jewish Christians who contemplated a retreat from Christianity and a return to Judaism with its sacrificial system. This is turning your back on Jesus.

"It has nothing to do with backsliders in our common use of that term. A man may be overtaken in a fault, or he may deliberately go into sin, and yet neither renounce the Gospel, nor deny the Lord that bought him. His case is dreary and dangerous, but it is not hopeless." - Adam Clarke

If Jesus' sacrifice for sin is rejected, there remains no other sacrifice that can cleanse. If someone does reject Jesus' sacrifice, fearful judgment is certain, even more than it was under the Old Covenant. It is fearful indeed to one-day face to God you have ultimately rejected and offended so greatly.

> Hebrews 10:32-34 (NASB) - "But remember the former days, when, after being enlightened, you endured a great conflict of sufferings, partly by being made a public spectacle through insults and distress, and partly by becoming companions with those who were so treated. For you showed sympathy to the prisoners and accepted joyfully the seizure of your property, knowing that you have for yourselves a better and lasting possession."

These Jewish Christians had already suffered for Jesus, being rejected from their Jewish community and perhaps being counted as dead. This came after they trusted in Jesus ("after being enlightened").

Their persecution was a struggle that came many different ways. They were "made a public spectacle through insults and distress". They were companians with others who were similarly treated. They also face economic persecution ("the seizure of your property"). But the point is that they had faced these things, and had endured them. They could take a look at their past endurance, and be encouraged to keep standing strong in the future.

They made is through the time of persecution by keeping a heavenly perspective. The writer to the Hebrews' point is clear: you can make it through this present time of discouragement also.

> Hebrews 10:35-38 (NASB) - "Therefore, do not throw away your confidence, which has a great reward. For you have need of endurance, so that when you have done the will of God, you may receive what was promised. For yet in a very little while, He who is coming will come, and will not delay. But My righteous one will live by faith; And if he shrinks back, My soul has no pleasure in him."

These discouraged Jewish Christians were in danger of casting away their confidence in Jesus, and relapsing into an Old Covenant relationship with God - one that is dependent on our human ability to follow the law.

"Do not throw it away…neither men nor devils can take it from you, and God will never deprive you of it if you continue faithful. There is a reference here to cowardly soldiers, who throw away their shields, and run away from the battle. This is your shield, your faith in Christ, which gives you the knowledge of salvation; keep it, and it will keep you." - Adam Clarke

They, and we, have need of endurance to receive the promise of God after we have done the will of God. The toughest and most discouraging trials are when we are called to obey God's will when the fulfillment of His promise seems so far away. This is why we need endurance. Faithfulness during the time when the promise seems unfulfilled is the measure of your obedience and spiritual maturity.

The writer of Hebrews quotes a passage from the book of Habakkuk:

> Habakkuk 2:4 (NASB) - "...But the righteous one will live by his faith."

We need to follow the footsteps of the righteous who will live by faith, and endure to see the promise fulfilled.

The writer of Hebrews gives a confident conclusion: 

> Hebrews 10:39 (NASB) - "But we are not among those who shrink back to destruction, but of those who have faith for the safekeeping of the soul."

We will not draw back into an Old Covenant relationship with God - or any other replacement for Jesus.
