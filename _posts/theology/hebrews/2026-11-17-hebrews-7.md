A Better Melchizedek

<br>

The writer of Hebrews now explains a theme that he/she introduced back in [Hebrews 5]({{ '/blog/hebrews/5' | relative_url }}):

> Hebrews 5:9-10 (NASB) - "And having been perfected, He [Jesus Christ] became the source of eternal salvation for all those who obey Him, being designated by God as High Priest according to the order of Melchizedek."

The Christian audience of Hebrews with a Jewish ancestry were interested in Jesus as their High Priest, but had significant objections because Jesus did not come from the tribe of Levi, or the priestly family of Aaron. According to Jewish law, only Aaronites (from Moses' brother Aaron), a family of the tribe of Levi, could be priests. For more on this, see [Numbers 18]({{ '/blog/numbers/18' | relative_url }}). Since Jesus is not a descendant of the family of Aaron, how could He be a High Priest?

The writer of Hebrews brilliantly connects this to a priesthood that predates the Levitical or Aaronite priesthood: the priesthood of Melchizedek, which we see in [Genesis 14]({{ '/blog/genesis/13-14' | relative_url }}):

> Genesis 14:18-20 (NASB) - "And Melchizedek the king of Salem brought out bread and wine; now he was a priest of God Most High. And he [Melchizedek] blessed him [Abram] and said, 'Blessed be Abram of God Most High, Possessor of heaven and earth; And blessed be God Most High, Who has handed over your enemies to you.' And he [Abram] gave him [Melchizedek] a tenth of everything."

Here's what the writer of Hebrews says about Melchizedek:

> Hebrews 7:1-3 (NASB) - "For this Melchizedek, king of Salem, priest of the Most High God, who met Abraham as he was returning from the slaughter of the kings and blessed him, to whom also Abraham apportioned a tenth of all the spoils, was first of all, by the translation of his name, king of righteousness, and then also king of Salem, which is king of peace. Without father, without mother, without genealogy, having neither beginning of days nor end of life, but made like the Son of God, he remains a priest perpetually."

Melchizedek:
* was called a "priest of God Most High"
* his name means "king of righteousness"
* he was the king of Salem (meaning "peace"), meaning that he could also be called the "king of peace"

> Hebrews 7:4-5 (NASB) - "Now observe how great this man was to whom Abraham, the patriarch, gave a tenth of the choicest spoils. And those indeed of the sons of Levi who receive the priest’s office have a commandment in the Law to collect a tenth from the people, that is, from their countrymen, although they are descended from Abraham."

> Genesis 14:20 (NASB) - "...And he [Abram] gave him [Melchizedek] a tenth of everything."

The law being referred to by the writer of Hebrews can be found in [Leviticus 27]({{ '/blog/leviticus/27' | relative_url }}) and Numbers 18:

> Leviticus 27:30,32 (NASB) - "Now all the tithe of the land, of the seed of the land or of the fruit of the tree, is the Lord’s; it is holy to the Lord...For every tenth part of herd or flock, whatever passes under the rod, the tenth one shall be holy to the Lord."

> Numbers 18:21,24 (NASB) - "To the sons of Levi, behold, I [the Lord] have given all the tithe in Israel as an inheritance, in return for their service which they perform, the service of the tent of meeting...For the tithe of the sons of Israel, which they offer as an offering to the Lord, I have given to the Levites as an inheritance..."

While this tithe from the Israelites to the Levites is commanded by the Law, the tithe given to Melchizedek by Abraham was voluntarily given by choice.

> Hebrews 7:6-7 (NASB) - "But the one whose genealogy is not traced from them collected a tenth from Abraham and blessed the one who had the promises. But without any dispute the lesser person is blessed by the greater."

There is nothing said about the geneology of Melchizedek in the Genesis 14 passage or anywhere else. As far as the Biblical record is concerned, he has no father or mother, no beginning of days nor end of life. The writer of Hebrews also writes that Melchizedek was greater than Abraham because he blessed Abraham, and the lesser person is blessed by the greater. On his part, Abraham accepted that Melchizedek was greater when he received the blessing.

> Hebrews 7:9-10 (NASB) - "And, so to speak, through Abraham even Levi, who received tithes, has paid tithes, for he was still in the loins of his forefather when Melchizedek met him."

Because the whole tribe of Levi was genetically in the loins of Abraham when he paid a tithe to Melchizedek, the writer of Hebrews uses this fact to show that even the priesthood of the Levites paid tithes to Melchizedek.

The writer raises another objection: If perfection could come through the Levitical priesthood (from which the people received the Law), there would be no need for another priesthood. Yet God described another priesthood later:

> Psalm 110:4 (NASB) - "The Lord has sworn and will not change His mind, 'You are a priest forever According to the order of Melchizedek.'"

The priesthood is different from the Levitical priesthood. The priesthood of Aaron was connected to the Law of Moses. So if the priesthood is changed we should also anticipate some change of the Law’s status or place. For Jesus Christ is a descendant of the tribe of Judah, a tribe which Moses did not connect with priesthood. This Priest, like Melchizedek, is not a priest because of geneology or ancestry, but because of another principle - a priesthood that lasted forever. This could never be said of a Levitical or Aaronite priest, who each served as priests but were limited to their own life spans. (15-17)

> Hebrews 7:18-19 (NASB) - "For, on the one hand, there is the nullification of a former commandment because of its weakness and uselessness (for the Law made nothing perfect); on the other hand, there is the introduction of a better hope, through which we come near to God."

The Law sets God's perfect standard, but it does not have the power to make anyone perfect. This is because the law is weak and useless when it comes to giving me power of sin. The law helps us recognize our sin, but it does not provide a cure - that is Jesus. Since now, in Jesus, we have a better hope, through which we draw near to God, we are wrong to go back to building our Christian walk on the law. Because we have a better High Priest, we also have a better hope and draw near to God. Our hope is in Jesus, not in the Law of Moses or our ability to keep it (see also [Galatians 3]({{ '/blog/galatians/3' | relative_url }})).

The writer then mentions how the priesthood of Jesus was established with an oath:

> Psalm 110:4 (NASB) - "The Lord has sworn and will not change His mind, 'You are a priest forever According to the order of Melchizedek.'"

The Aaronite priesthood was not established by an oath from God. God sealed His choice of Jesus as a better High Priest by an oath. (20-21)

The Old Covenant had a mediator (Moses), but no one to guarantee the people's side of the covenant. Therefore they continually failed under it. But the New Covenant - a better covenant - has Jesus as a guarantee (or surety) on our behalf. Therefore, the New Covenant depends on what Jesus did, not on what we do. He is the guarantee and we are not. The overwhelming superiority of Jesus Christ proves He is worthy and able to be our guarantee, our cosigner of a better covenant. (22)

The former priesthood was constantly changing, as the priests were limited by their human life spans. Jesus however, holds the priesthood permanently, because He lives forever.

> Hebrews 7:25 (NASB) - "Therefore He is also able to save forever those who come to God through Him, since He always lives to make intercession for them."

The writer of Hebrews ends this chapter by summarizing everything he/she has written thus far:

> Hebrews 7:26-28 (NASB) - "For it was fitting for us to have such a high priest [Jesus], holy, innocent, undefiled, separated from sinners, and exalted above the heavens; who has no daily need, like those [Levitical] high priests, to offer up sacrifices, first for His own sins and then for the sins of the people, because He [Jesus] did this once for all time when He offered up Himself. For the Law appoints men as high priests who are weak, but the word of the oath, which came after the Law, appoints a Son, who has been made perfect forever."
