## Warnings

> Hebrews 6:1-2 (NASB) - "Therefore leaving the elementary teaching about the Christ, let us press on to maturity, not laying again a foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God, of instruction about washings and laying on of hands, and about the resurrection of the dead and eternal judgment."

This list mentions:
* repentance from dead works 
* faith toward God
* instruction about washings
* laying on of hands
* resurrection of the dead
* eternal judgment

This list is important to understand! The writer of Hebrews is not saying that we should throw these principles out, but we should first understand them and then seek to build on them.

"When we consider the 'rudiments' one by one, it is remarkable how little in the list is distinctive of Christianity, for practically every item could have its place in a fairly orthodox Jewish community... Each of them, indeed, acquires a new significance in a Christian context; but the impression we get is that existing Jewish beliefs and practices were used as a foundation on which to build Christian truth." - A.B. Bruce

"washings" = *baptismos* - Strong's Concordance [909]
* Jewish ceremonial washings
* Not baptism in a Christian sense

These "elementary principles" were a safe common ground between Judaism and Christianity. It was easy for a Jewish Christian to slip back into Judaism in order to avoid persecution. A Jew and a Christian together could say, "Let's repent, let's have faith." But this was a subtle yet certain denial of Jesus. The writer of Hebrews is encouraging these Jewish Christians to "press on to maturity", moving on to something better.

> Hebrews 6:3 (NASB) - "And this we will do, if God permits."

This is not implying that God may not want them to go on to maturity, past those basics common to Christianity and Judaism. Instead, this expresses the believers' complete dependence on God. If we do press on to maturity, we realize that it only happens at God's pleasure.

Certainly God wants us to press on to maturity, but our spiritual condition may be hindering us, as the writer of Hebrews was saying at the end of [Hebrews 5]({{ '/blog/hebrews/5' | relative_url }}):

> Hebrews 5:11 (NASB) - "Concerning him we have much to say, and it is difficult to explain, since you have become poor listeners."

The writer then moves on:

> Hebrews 6:4-6 (NASB) - "For it is impossible, in the case of those who have once been enlightened and have tasted of the heavenly gift and have been made partakers of the Holy Spirit, and have tasted the good word of God and the powers of the age to come, and then have fallen away, to restore them again to repentance, since they again crucify to themselves the Son of God and put Him to open shame."

Notice the word "impossible" - it is not merely difficult, but without possibility

The writer mentions:
* those who have been enlightened
* those who have tasted of the heavenly gift
* those who have been made partakers of the Holy Spirit
* those who have tasted the good word of God and the powers of the age to come

If one of these persons then has fallen away, it is impossible to restore them to repentance.

This passage is heavily debated. The question is simple: Are these people with these impressive spiritual experiences in fact Christians?

It is certainly possible that someone can have a great spiritual experience and still not be saved:

> Matthew 7:21-23 (NASB) - "Not everyone who says to Me, 'Lord, Lord,' will enter the kingdom of heaven, but the one who does the will of My Father who is in heaven will enter. Many will say to Me on that day, 'Lord, Lord, did we not prophesy in Your name, and in Your name cast out demons, and in Your name perform many miracles?' And then I will declare to them, 'I never knew you; leave Me, you who practice lawlessness.'"

Yet from human observation (and our inability to see exactly what another person believes in their heart), we must assume that the author of Hebrews is speaking about Christians in verses 4-6. It seems to me that the writer of Hebrews is writing directly about the Jewish Christians he/she said were spiritually immature (see [Hebrews 5]({{ '/blog/hebrews/5' | relative_url }})). Remember, these Jewish Christians were considering giving up on Jesus (or, becoming unbelievers) because of the persecution they suffered.

> Hebrews 6:7-8 - "For ground that drinks the rain which often falls on it and produces vegetation useful to those for whose sake it is also tilled, receives a blessing from God; but if it yields thorns and thistles, it is worthless and close to being cursed, and it ends up being burned."

The writer of Hebrews, speaking to the Jewish Christians, is essentially saying: "You guys are the land, there is seed being planted in your life, and so how you choose to handle that seed (what you do), especially in times of suffering, really matters!"

We see that this difficult passage is best understood in its context. The writer to the Hebrews means that if they retreat from Christianity back to Judaism, forsaking Jesus, then they are essentially crucifying Him again and denying His work for them on the cross.

> Romans 4:4-5 (NASB) - "Now to the one who works, the wages are not credited as a favor, but as what is due. But to the one who does not work, but believes in Him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is credited as righteousness,"

> Titus 3:5 (NASB) - "He saved us, not on the basis of deeds which we did in righteousness, but in accordance with His mercy..."

If someone does in fact "fall away" from Christianity, we must understand why it is " impossible" for them to repent (see verses 4-6) - it is because they don't want to. God does not prohibit their repentance. The desire to repent is evidence that he or she has not truly fallen away, as repentance itself is a work of God in a person:

> Romans 2:4 (NASB) - "Or do you think lightly of the riches of His kindness and restraint and patience, not knowing that the kindness of God leads you to repentance?"

## Better Things Ahead

> Hebrews 6:9-10 (NASB) - "But, beloved, we are convinced of better things regarding you, and things that accompany salvation, even though we are speaking in this way. For God is not unjust so as to forget your work and the love which you have shown toward His name, by having served and by still serving the saints."

Here the writer of Hebrews expresses confidence that His readers would continue on with Jesus. He thinks of their continuation in the faith as one of the things that accompany salvation.

When we are suffering or discouraged we often think that God has abandoned us or forgot about us and all we have done for Him. But God would deny His own nature if He forgot such things, making Him unjust. God sees us and remembers our faithfulness and obedience to Him.

The writer to the Hebrews then encouraged them to press on in the faith until death, and to become imitators of other believers, who through faith and endurance inherit (not earn) God's promises. One example of this is Abraham, whom God swore to saying, "Indeed I will greatly bless you and I will greatly multiply you". Having patiently endured, Abraham obtained the promise. We also should be encouraged to hold firmly on to the hope and promises of God to us, because it is impossible for God to lie. (11-18)

> Hebrews 6:19-20 (NASB) - "This hope we have as an anchor of the soul, a hope both sure and reliable and one which enters within the veil, where Jesus has entered as a forerunner for us, having become a high priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek."

An anchor was a common figure for hope in the ancient world, bringing the idea that we are anchored to something firm but unseen. The Holy of Holies (the Most Holy Place) was guarded and separated from the Holy Place by a veil (see [Exodus 26]({{ '/blog/exodus/25-27' | relative_url }})):

> Exodus 26:31-33 (NASB) - "You shall also make a veil of violet, purple, and scarlet material, and fine twisted linen... You shall hang up the veil under the clasps, and bring in the ark of the testimony there within the veil; and the veil shall serve as a partition for you between the Holy Place and the Most Holy Place."

On the Day of Atonement ([Leviticus 16]({{ '/blog/leviticus/16' | relative_url }})), the High Priest would enter the Most Holy Place to make atonement for the sins of the people. The writer of Hebrews connects this with Jesus, who has entered the veil for us. We should be anchored to Jesus, who behind the veil acts as our forerunner (implying that we then should follow Him as the after-runners).

The temple analogy (behind the veil) reminds the writer to the Hebrews of his previous start into the subject of Jesus as our High Priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek (back in [Hebrews 5]({{ '/blog/hebrews/5' | relative_url }})).
