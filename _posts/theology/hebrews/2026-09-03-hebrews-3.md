Superior to Moses

<br>

> Hebrews 3:1 (NASB) - "Therefore, holy brothers and sisters, partakers of a heavenly calling, consider the Apostle and High Priest of our confession: Jesus;"

The NIV translation says "fix your thoughts on..."

The writer of Hebrews then compares Jesus Christ with Moses. Just as Moses was faithful in "his house", so also Christ was faithful. The "house" refers to God's people, meaning the community of believers, often referred to as "the children of God". Moses was a member of this house, but Jesus Christ is worthy of more glory because He is the builder of the house. (2-4)

> Hebrews 3:5 (NASB) - "Now Moses was faithful in all God’s house as a servant, for a testimony of those things which were to be spoken later;"

Moses was a servant, and his faith was a testimony of "those things which were to be spoken after". What are those future things?

The future things, or "things which were to be spoken after" Moses, refer to the message of the gospel and the full revelation of Jesus Christ.

> Hebrews 3:6 (NASB) - "but Christ was faithful as a Son over His house—whose house we are, if we hold firmly to our confidence and the boast of our hope."

We (believers) are members of the house of God, meaning the community of believers, if we hold fast to the confidence and hope we have in Christ until our death. We can rejoice in our hope and confidence because of our salvation!

> Hebrews 3:7-11 (NASB) - "Therefore, just as the Holy Spirit says, 'Today if you hear His voice, Do not harden your hearts as when they provoked Me, As on the day of trial in the wilderness, Where your fathers put Me to the test, And saw My works for forty years. Therefore I was angry with this generation, And said, 'They always go astray in their heart,
And they did not know My ways'; As I swore in My anger, 'They certainly shall not enter My rest.'"

This is a direct quote from Psalm 95, and refers to the generation of Moses whom God took out of slavery in Egypt, and who wandered in the desert for 40 years. Because of their grumbling and testing of the Lord, we see in [Numbers 14]({{ '/blog/numbers/14' | relative_url }}) that the Lord does not allow them to enter "my rest", meaning the Promised Land.

> Numbers 14:21-23 (NASB) - "however, as I live, all the earth will be filled with the glory of the Lord. Certainly all the people who have seen My glory and My signs which I performed in Egypt and in the wilderness, yet have put Me to the test these ten times and have not listened to My voice, shall by no means see the land which I swore to their fathers, nor shall any of those who were disrespectful to Me see it."

> Numbers 14:29-30 (NASB) - "your dead bodies will fall in this wilderness, all your numbered men according to your complete number from twenty years old and upward, who have grumbled against Me. By no means will you come into the land where I swore to settle you"

Looking back at Hebrews 3, we see a repetition of the same concepts in verses 15-19. Bible scholars have identified this to be a chiasm:

* A: "hold fast the confidence and the rejoicing of hope" (6)
* B: "today if you will hear his voice" (7)
* C: "harden not your hearts, as in the provocation" (8)
* D: "your fathers tempted me, proved me" (9)
* E: grief (10)
* F: "they shall not enter into my rest" (11)
* A': "hold the beginning of our confidence steadfast" (14)
* B': "today if you will hear his voice" (15)
* C': "harden not your hearts, as in the provocation" (15)
* D': "for some, when they had heard, did provoke" (16)
* E': grief (17)
* F': "they should not enter into his rest" (18)

As we have seen with chiasms, what is in the middle of the two halves tends to be most important - in this case verses 12 and 13.

> Hebrews 3:12-13 (NASB) - "Take care, brothers and sisters, that there will not be in any one of you an evil, unbelieving heart that falls away from the living God. But encourage one another every day, as long as it is still called “today,” so that none of you will be hardened by the deceitfulness of sin."

We see a call to "take heed", lest there be any unbelief, and to exhort one another daily. It appears that the author of Hebrews is taking a concept that had a previous application and giving it another application for today. There is a former, physical application that refers to Ancient Israel. There is also a new, spiritual application that applies to believers.

| | The Former Application | The New Application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Leader:** | Moses | Jesus |
| **The "Today":** | A specific day of testing in the wilderness | Any day still called "today" (the present time) |
| **The Danger:** | A hardened heart that led to physical rebellion | An unbelieving heart that leads a person to fall away from the living God |
| **The "Rest":** | Entry into the Promised Land of Canaan | A spiritual fellowship with God through Christ |

The writer of Hebrews will expound more on this comparison in the next chapter.
