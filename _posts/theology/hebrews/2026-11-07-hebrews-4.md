Sabbath Rest

<br>

> Hebrews 4:1 (NASB) - "Therefore, we must fear if, while a promise remains of entering His rest, any one of you may seem to have come short of it."

Again we see the word "therefore", denoting a continuation of the pervious conversation. In the previous chapter ([Hebrews 3]({{ '/blog/hebrews/3'}})), the author of Hebrews made a parallel between the Promised Land and the rest found by a believer in Jesus Christ. Just as an entire generation of Israelites were unable to enter the Promised Land because of their unbelief, Christians today should take heed that we continuously receive the gospel by faith and believe.

> Hebrews 4:2-3 (NASB) - "For indeed we have had good news preached to us, just as they also did; but the word they heard did not benefit them, because they were not united with those who listened with faith. For we who have believed enter that rest, just as He has said, 'As I swore in My anger, They certainly shall not enter My rest,' although His works were finished from the foundation of the world."

They which believe by faith do enter into rest! This is a quote from Psalm 95:

> Psalm 95:11 (NASB) - "Therefore I swore in My anger, They certainly shall not enter My rest."

Notice this verse says "my rest" - the rest is God's. Although the works have been finished since creation, God still has this rest.

> Hebrews 4:4 (NASB) - "For He has said somewhere concerning the seventh day: 'And God rested on the seventh day from all His works';"

> Genesis 2:2 (NASB) - "By the seventh day God completed His work which He had done, and He rested on the seventh day from all His work which He had done."

Some will enter God's rest through faith, others will not enter because of unbelief. If Israel (those to whom it was first preached) failed to enter because of their unbelief, then someone else would enter into that rest because of their belief. (5-6)

> Hebrews 4:7-8 (NASB) - "He again sets a certain day, 'Today,' saying through David after so long a time just as has been said before, 'Today if you hear His voice, Do not harden your hearts.' For if Joshua had given them rest, He would not have spoken of another day after that."

> Psalm 95:7-8 (NASB) - "...Today, if you will hear His voice, Do not harden your hearts..."

The presence of these verses in Psalms prove that there is still rest to be found, rest that is beyond the physical fulfillment of entering into the Promised Land. If the Israelites completely fulfilled the promise of rest by entering into the land, this appeal in Psalms (written much later by David) with the word "today" would make no sense.

> Hebrews 4:9 (NASB) - "Consequently, there remains a Sabbath rest for the people of God."

If there remains a rest for the people of God, then it must mean that there are some who must spiritually enter it. If a generation of Israelites failed to enter the Promised Land because of their disobedience, then someone else (Joshua and the next generation) would enter. 

The names "Joshua" and "Jesus" are derived from the same Hebrew root name, although they appear in slightly different forms:
* The name "Joshua" comes from the full Hebrew name *Yehoshua*, meaning "Yahweh saves" or "Yahweh is salvation"
* The name "Jesus" comes from *Yeshua*, which is the standard shorter form of *Yehoshua* (much like "Joe" is to "Joseph")

When the Hebrew Old Testament was translated into Greek (the Septuagint) and when the New Testament was written in Greek, both "Yehoshua" and "Yeshua" were rendered into the same Greek name *Iēsous*. This eventually evolved into the English name Jesus after the letter "J" came into use.

Just as God rested from His work on the seventh day of creation (Genesis 2:2), so also we should rest from our works. This means there is no longer any place for works as a basis for our own righteousness, fulfilling our "Sabbath rest."

"There is a sense in which to enter Christian salvation means to cease from one’s works and rest securely on what Christ has done." - Leon Morris

We must make every effort to enter into God's spiritual rest. The rest is there, but God does not force it upon us. The faith needed to enter this rest is not passive, as it is part of the sanctification process. If we are not diligent to enter the spiritual rest promised to us, we may fall and fail to enter that rest in our lifetime, just like the generation of Israelites who hardened their hearts and failed to enter into the Promised Land. 

> Hebrews 4:12-13 (NASB) - "For the word of God is living and active, and sharper than any two-edged sword, even penetrating as far as the division of soul and spirit, of both joints and marrow, and able to judge the thoughts and intentions of the heart. And there is no creature hidden from His sight, but all things are open and laid bare to the eyes of Him to whom we must answer."

God's Word accurately diagnoses our spiritual health. We can not pretend to believe, while actually harboring unbelief in our hearts, for the word of God is able to judge our thoughts and intentions. It is important to submit ourselves to the Word of God, to discern and root up any false beliefs, allowing the Holy Spirit to work through us and transform us by the renewing of our minds:

> Romans 12:2 (NASB) - "And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, so that you may prove what the will of God is, that which is good and acceptable and perfect."

The writer to the Hebrews trusts that he has pierced the hearts of his audience, who thought about "giving up" on Jesus. In this passage, he makes it clear that they can't give up on Jesus and keep it hidden from God. The Word of God discovers and exposes their condition - the Word is Jesus Christ.

> Hebrews 4:14-16 (NASB) - "Therefore, since we have a great high priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let’s hold firmly to our confession."

The idea that Jesus is our High Priest was mentioned before in Hebrews 2:17 and Hebrews 3:1. I also mentioned this idea in my [Leviticus 16]({{ '/blog/leviticus/16' | relative_url}}) post. But now the idea will be developed more extensively.

The writer to the Hebrews calls attention to the unique character of Jesus as our High Priest:
* No other High Priest was called great.
* No other High Priest passed through the heavens.
* No other High Priest is the Son of God.

These truths should encourage us to hold fast to our confession of faith.

> Hebrews 4:15 (NASB) - "For we do not have a high priest who cannot sympathize with our weaknesses, but One who has been tempted in all things just as we are, yet without sin."

Thus far, the writer to the Hebrews was careful to document both the deity and humanity of Jesus. Because of His humanity, it means that Jesus, our High Priest, can sympathize with our weaknesses. To ancient Greeks, the primary attribute of God was *apatheia*, the essential inability to feel anything at all. Jesus isn't like that - He knows and feels what we go through, suffering along with us.

Jesus never faced temptation in an inner sense the way we do, because there was never a sinful nature pulling Him to sin from the inside. But He knew the strength of temptation from outside forces in a way and to a degree that we can never know. He knows what we go through and He has faced worse, yet He endured triumphantly and never sinned.

> Hebrews 4:16 (NASB) - "Therefore let’s approach the throne of grace with confidence, so that we may receive mercy and find grace for help at the time of our need."

mercy = not getting what we deserve
grace = getting what we don't deserve

Mercy removes the penalty we deserve, while grace gives us the blessings of salvation that we didn't earn.
