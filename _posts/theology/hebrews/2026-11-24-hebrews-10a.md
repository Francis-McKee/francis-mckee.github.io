A Better Sacrifice

<br>

> Hebrews 10:1-4 (NASB) - "For the Law, since it has only a shadow of the good things to come and not the form of those things itself, can never, by the same sacrifices which they offer continually every year, make those who approach perfect. Otherwise, would they not have ceased to be offered, because the worshipers, having once been cleansed, would no longer have had consciousness of sins? But in those sacrifices there is a reminder of sins every year. For it is impossible for the blood of bulls and goats to take away sins."

The sacrifices made under the Old Covenant of bulls and goats could not make a person perfect. A person would not need to offer sacrifices for sin continuously if they were made perfect after the first sacrifice. The repetition of sacrifice shows its inherent weakness. If animal sacrifices had solved the sin problem, then they would have ceased to be offered.

Rather than taking away sin, every repeated sacrifice was a reminder of sins. It brought the consciousness of sins to the people again and again.

"All they are is a reminder of sin. So far from purifying a man, they remind him that he is not purified and that his sins still stand between him and God." - William Barclay

> Hebrews 10:5-7 (NASB) - "Therefore, when He comes into the world, He says, 'You have not desired sacrifice and offering, But You have prepared a body for Me; You have not taken pleasure in whole burnt offerings and offerings for sin. Then I said, "Behold, I have come (It is written of Me in the scroll of the book) To do Your will, O God."'"

This quotation is taken from the Septuagint version of Psalm 40:6-8. The Septuagint is the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament that was the most commonly used Bible in the first century. It shows that Jesus prophetically declared the insufficient character of Old Covenant sacrifice and declared His willingness to offer a perfect sacrifice under the New Covenant.

> Psalm 40:6-8 (NASB) - "You have not desired sacrifice and meal offering; You have opened my ears; You have not required burnt offering and sin offering. Then I said, 'Behold, I have come; It is written of me in the scroll of the book. I delight to do Your will, my God; Your Law is within my heart.'"

"The text of the LXX [Septuagint] is followed in the main which differs from the Hebrew chiefly in having *sōma* (body) rather than *ōtia* (ears)." - Archibald Robertson

More animal sacrifice, made under the law, would not please God. In the Old Testament, God repeatedly expressed His desire for obedience rather than for more sacrifice.

> 1 Samuel 15:22 (NASB) - "Samuel said, 'Does the Lord have as much delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices As in obeying the voice of the Lord? Behold, to obey is better than a sacrifice, And to pay attention is better than the fat of rams.'"

> Hosea 6:6 (NASB) - "For I [the Lord] desire loyalty rather than sacrifice, And the knowledge of God rather than burnt offerings."

Instead, what pleased God could only come through Jesus, the incarnate Son of God. In the incarnation the body of Jesus was perfectly prepared before the foundation of the world and was suited to live as fully man and fully God, according to the will of God.

> Hebrews 10:10 (NASB) - "By this will, we have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all time."

Again, notice the importance of the phrase "once for all time."

> Hebrews 10:11-13 (NASB) - "Every priest stands daily ministering and offering time after time the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins; but He, having offered one sacrifice for sins for all time, sat down at the right hand of God, waiting from that time onward until His enemies are made a footstool for His feet."

The priests had to stand continually in their work. Their work continued daily and sacrifices had to be repeatedly offered. The priests could never sit down! In contrast, Jesus sat down at the right hand of God, having finished His work by coming to earth and dying on the cross as one sacrifice for sin.

The seated posture of Jesus is important, as it shows that His work is finished. He doesn't need to stand ministering daily and offering the same sacrifices over and over. (as priests under the Old Covenant had to do). Jesus still ministers in heaven - He has a ministry of intercession for His people. But that ministry flows from His completed work, so He can adopt a posture of rest, till "His enemies are made a footstool for His feet."

## Conclusion of Hebrews *Inclusio*

It is here at the end of the *inclusio* of Hebrews that we come to the conclusion of the doctrinal arguement the writer is making: there no longer must be an offering for sin. The writer of Hebrews then spends some time explaining this truth:

> Hebrews 10:14 (NASB) - "For by one offering He has perfected for all time those who are sanctified."

This makes it clear that the work of Jesus is effective only for those who are being sanctified. The work of Jesus is capable for the justification of every human being who believes, but it is effective in saving those who have been justified and now are being sanctified. Only those who believe in Jesus and His finished work can have true atonement for sins and be called perfect (complete/whole).

"What a glorious word! Those for whom Christ has died were perfected by his death. It does not mean that he made them perfect in characters so that they are no longer sinners, but that he made those for whom he died perfectly free from the guilt of sin. When Christ took their sins upon himself, sin remained no longer upon them, for it could not be in two places at one and the same time." - Charles Spurgeon

> Hebrews 10:15-18 (NASB) - "And the Holy Spirit also testifies to us; for after saying, 'This is the covenant which I will make with them After those days, declares the Lord: I will put My laws upon their hearts, And write them on their mind,' He then says, 'And their sins and their lawless deeds I will no longer remember.' Now where there is forgiveness of these things, an offering for sin is no longer required."

The writer of Hebrews is quoting a passage from the book of Jeremiah:

> Jeremiah 31:33-34 - "'For this is the covenant which I will make with the house of Israel after those days,' declares the Lord: 'I will put My law within them and write it on their heart; and I will be their God, and they shall be My people. They will not teach again, each one his neighbor and each one his brother, saying, "Know the Lord," for they will all know Me, from the least of them to the greatest of them,' declares the Lord, 'for I will forgive their wrongdoing, and their sin I will no longer remember.'"

The New Covenant has to do with inner transformation. God changes the heart of man, and writes His law into their hearts.

The New Covenant offers complete forgiveness. The forgiveness is so complete that God can say that He doesn't even remember our sins in light of the New Covenant! The Christian must endeavor to do with their sin exactly what God has done: forget about it. As well, this reminds us that a person's past sin has no bearing on God's present dealings with him/her (not a cause and effect relationship).

Where sins are really forgiven and forgotten (remission), there no longer must be an offering for sin. 

"Forgiveness of sin is the characteristic of the new covenant. In Jeremiah complete pardon of sins is promised. If the pardon is complete, there is left no place for the Levitical sacrifices under the new covenant." - Marvin Vincent

What follows after the inclusio is mainly an exhortation showing us how we should live in light of this core gospel truth.
