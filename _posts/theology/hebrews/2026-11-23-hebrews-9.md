The New Covenant

<br>

> Hebrews 9:1-5 (NASB) - "Now even the first covenant had regulations for divine worship and the earthly sanctuary. For a tabernacle was equipped, the outer sanctuary, in which were the lampstand, the table, and the sacred bread; this is called the Holy Place. Behind the second veil there was a tabernacle which is called the Most Holy Place, having a golden altar of incense and the ark of the covenant covered on all sides with gold, in which was a golden jar holding the manna, Aaron’s staff which budded [[Numbers 17]({{ '/blog/numbers/17' | relative_url }})], and the tablets of the covenant; and above it were the cherubim of glory overshadowing the atoning cover; but about these things we cannot now speak in detail."

**Old Covenant Atonement:**

On the Day of Atonement, the blood of sacrifice was spinkled upon the mercy sit (on the lid of the ark of the covenant) for the forgiveness of Israel's sins. As God looked down into the ark, He saw the symbols of Israel's sin, rebellion, and failure. But when the blood of sacrifice was applied to the mercy seat, the blood of sacrifice covered His sight of the sin of Israel. 

The priests, as appointed, went daily into the Holy Place to perform priestly functions such as tending the lampstand and replacing the showbread. (6)

> Hebrews 9:9-10 (NASB) - "...Accordingly both gifts and sacrifices are offered which cannot make the worshiper perfect in conscience, since they relate only to food, drink, and various washings, regulations for the body imposed until a time of reformation."

But the High Priest only entered the Most Holy Place (sometimes called the "Holy of Holies") once a year, on the Day of Atonement. The High Priest entered the Most Holy Place with blood to atone first for his own sins and then for the sins of the people. The focus of this atonement was sins that were committed unknowingly. (7)

> Hebrews 9:18-22 (NASB) - "Therefore even the first covenant was not inaugurated without blood. For when every commandment had been spoken by Moses to all the people according to the Law, he took the blood of the calves and the goats, with water and scarlet wool and hyssop, and sprinkled both the book itself and all the people, saying, 'This is the blood of the covenant which God commanded you.' And in the same way he sprinkled both the tabernacle and all the vessels of the ministry with the blood. And almost all things are cleansed with blood, according to the Law, and without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness."

Clearly, death (animal sacrifice) was necessary to the Old Covenant. Virtually every part of the sacrificial system under the Law of Moses was touched by blood in some way. Under the Old Covenant, without the shedding of blood, there is no forgiveness for sins.

**New Covenant Atonement:**

> Hebrews 9:8-9 (NASB) - "The Holy Spirit is signifying this, that the way into the holy place has not yet been disclosed while the outer tabernacle is still standing, which is a symbol for the present time..."

The Old Covenant had to disappear before the New Covenant could be revealed. The Temple had to be destroyed by the Romans in 70 AD before God's new way was revealed. The Tabernacle/Temple itself and all that the Old Covenant represented were symbolic of deeper truths of the New Covenant:

> Hebrews 9:11-14 (NASB) - "But when Christ appeared as a high priest of the good things having come, He entered through the greater and more perfect tabernacle, not made by hands, that is, not of this creation; and not through the blood of goats and calves, but through His own blood, He entered the holy place once for all time, having obtained eternal redemption. For if the blood of goats and bulls, and the ashes of a heifer sprinkling those who have been defiled, sanctify for the cleansing of the flesh, how much more will the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered Himself without blemish to God, cleanse your conscience from dead works to serve the living God?"

Jesus, as our High Priest, ministers in a superior sanctuary - the very throne room of God. This is obviously a place greater than anything human hands could make.

Animal sacrifice was sufficient for a temporary covering of sin, but only a perfect sacrifice could obtain eternal redemption. Jesus' sacrifice was superior in that it was perfect, voluntary, rational, and motivated by love.

At the Tabernacle, under the Old Covenant, the sacrifice for atonement was made outside the Most Holy Place, but the blood was brought in to the Most Holy Place by the High Priest. In the same pattern, under the New Covenant, Jesus had to die here on eath, outside Heaven, and among sinful men, but the payment His death made was satisfied in Heaven itself.

Under the Old Covenant, the High Priest entered the Most Holy Place once a year, going through the veil and back again - the barrier remained. When Jesus died, He tore the veil, welcoming us in to the Most Holy Place, not so we can offer additional atonement for sins, but so we can have fellowship with God.

> Mark 15:37-38 (NASB) - "But Jesus let out a loud cry, and died. And the veil of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom."

The sacrifice of Jesus is sufficient to atone not just for sins we have unknowingly commited, but to even restore our damaged conscience for sins we are aware of. The idea behind "dead works" in verse 14, probably speaks of sinful action in general, in the sense of "works that bring death." But it must also speak to the vain continuation of Old Covenant sacrifice, which is certainly a dead work - and the very type of thing the audience of the book of Hebrews (discouraged Jewish Christians) were tempted to go back to.

Jesus' work as the Mediator of the New Covenant is fundamentally accomplished at His death. His payment on the cross accomplished redemption for those under the first covenant. Every sacrifice for sin made in faith under the Mosaic command was paid in full at the cross. (15)

> Hebrews 9:16-17 (NASB) - "For where there is a covenant, there must of necessity be the death of the one who made it. For a covenant is valid only when people are dead, for it is never in force while the one who made it lives."

A covenant (some translations say "testament"), in the sense of a last will and testament, only takes effect when the person who made the testament dies. Therefore Jesus had to die for the New Testament (New Covenant) to take effect.

"If there be a question about whether a man is alive or not, you cannot administer to his estate, but when you have certain evidence that the testator has died then the will stands. So is it with the blessed gospel: if Jesus did not die, then the gospel is null and void." - Charles Spurgeon

> Hebrews 9:23-28 (NASB) - "Therefore it was necessary for the copies of the things in the heavens to be cleansed with these things [the blood of calves and goats], but the heavenly things themselves with better sacrifices [the blood of Jesus Christ] than these."

Jesus Christ is a better sacrifice than the blood of calves and goats.

> Hebrews 9:24-26 (NASB) - "For Christ did not enter a holy place made by hands, a mere copy of the true one, but into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God for us; nor was it that He would offer Himself often, as the high priest enters the Holy Place year by year with blood that is not his own. Otherwise, He would have needed to suffer often since the foundation of the world; but now once at the consummation of the ages He has been revealed to put away sin by the sacrifice of Himself."

Jesus' sacrifice for our sake was made on earth, but His ministry for our sake as our Mediator and High Priest continues on in Heaven.His ministry is not continuing in the sense of offering atonement for sin, but in intercession and defending us against the accuser of God's people:

> Revelation 12:10 (NASB) - "Then I heard a loud voice in heaven, saying, 'Now the salvation, and the power, and the kingdom of our God and the authority of His Christ have come, for the accuser of our brothers and sisters has been thrown down, the one who accuses them before our God day and night."

Imperfect sacrifices must be repeated continually but a perfect sacrifice can be made once for all time, and genuinely "put away sin" - not just cleanse the outside of a person without dealing with a person's heart, as with sacrifice under the Old Covenant. The message is clear: Jesus has appeared on earth to put away sin by the sacrifice of Himself.

> Hebrews 9:27-28 (NASB) - "And just as it is destined for people to die once, and after this comes judgment, so Christ also, having been offered once to bear the sins of many, will appear a second time for salvation without reference to sin, to those who eagerly await Him."

It is customary for a person to die once (minus exceptions such as Enoch and Elijah), and then face judgment for their life on earth. The focus of Jesus' first coming on earth was to deal with the problem of sin through His atoning sacrifice. But now, having dealt with the problem of sin perfectly, once for all time, He will come again - for the salvation (in the sense of rescue) of His people who eagerly await Him.
