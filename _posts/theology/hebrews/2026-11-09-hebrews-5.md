A Better High Priest

<br>

Hebrews 5 continues the conversation from the previous chapter:

> Hebrews 5:1-3 (NASB) - "For every high priest taken from among men is appointed on behalf of people in things pertaining to God, in order to offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins; he can deal gently with the ignorant and misguided, since he himself also is clothed in weakness; and because of it he is obligated to offer sacrifices for sins for himself, as well as for the people."

See [Exodus 28]({{ '/blog/exodus/28-30' | relative_url }}) and onward for the establishment of the priesthood and the office of High Priest.

The writer to the Hebrews summarizes the work of the High Priest, in saying that he may "offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins." Being "clothed in weakness" means being weak in nature, as in being human or mortal and having an internal sin nature. Because every High Priest understands that they also are "clothed in weakness", they are able to have compassion on other people in order to minister the atoning sacrifices. On the Day of Atonement (see [Leviticus 16]({{ '/blog/leviticus/16' | relative_url }})), the High Priest had to sacrifice for himself first, to remind himself and the nation that he had sin to atone for, just like the rest of the people of Israel.

The High Priest was chosen and appointed by God, given by right of birth. It was an honor no man could take to himself. God set the tribe of Levi apart as a tribe to serve Him (see [Numbers 3]({{ '/blog/numbers/3' | relative_url }})). Levi had three sons, whose family lines served in different roles. These families were not properly priests, though they were Levites. The priesthood itself came through Aaron, a Levite and the brother of Moses. Aaron's family and descendants made up the priest and the High Priest, those able to serve in the tabernacle itself and to offer sacrifice to God. In this sense, the priesthood was not appointed by man, but by God. (4)

> Hebrews 5:5-6 (NASB) - "So too Christ did not glorify Himself in becoming a high priest, but it was He who said to Him, 'You are My Son, Today I have fathered You'; just as He also says in another passage, 'You are a priest forever According to the order of Melchizedek.'"

Jesus did not make Himself High Priest. Instead, just as much as Jesus was declared to be the Son (in Psalm 2:7), He was also delcared to be a priest forever (in Psalm 110:4):

> Psalm 2:7 (NASB) - "I will announce the decree of the Lord: He said to Me, 'You are My Son, Today I have fathered You.'"

> Psalm 110:4 (NASB) - "The Lord has sworn and will not change His mind, 'You are a priest forever According to the order of Melchizedek.'"

It is easy to see why the priesthood of Jesus was difficult for early Jewish Christians to grasp: Jesus was not from the lineage of Aaron. 

> Hebrews 5:7-8 (NASB) - "In the days of His humanity, He offered up both prayers and pleas with loud crying and tears to the One able to save Him from death, and He was heard because of His devout behavior. Although He was a Son, He learned obedience from the things which He suffered."

The agony of Jesus in the Garden of Gesthemane proved that He struggled with the difficulty of obedience, yet He obeyed perfectly:

> Luke 22:44 (NASB) - "And being in agony, He was praying very fervently; and His sweat became like drops of blood, falling down upon the ground]."

God, enthroned in Heaven, did not know the experience of obedience. But He cast Himself off the throne of Heaven (as Jesus), enduring suffering in order to learn the experience of obedience. It is important to clarify - Jesus did not learn how to obey, passing from disobedience to obedience. Jesus learned the experience of obedience, which God could not have had unless He came down from Heaven.

"Obedience is a trade to which a man must be apprenticed until he has learned it, for it is not to be known in any other way. Even our blessed Lord could not have fully learned obedience by the observation in others of such an obedience as he had personally to render, for there was no one from whom he could thus learn." - Charles Spurgeon

> Hebrews 5:9-10 (NASB) - "And having been perfected, He became the source of eternal salvation for all those who obey Him, being designated by God as High Priest according to the order of Melchizedek."

Notice in Psalm 110:4 (above), it says "You [Jesus] are a priest forever". Jesus' ressurection from the dead demonstrated that His priesthood is unending. His life of obedience demonstrated that Jesus was not a priest like Aaron, who had to atone for his own sin first. Rather, Jesus was bore the wrath sinners deserved without becoming a sinner Himself.

The story of Melchizedek appears in [Genesis 14]({{ '/blog/genesis/13-14' | relative_url }}), and the writer of Hebrews will more fully develop the connection to Melchizedek later on.

> Hebrews 5:11-14 (NASB) - "Concerning him we have much to say, and it is difficult to explain, since you have become poor listeners. For though by this time you ought to be teachers, you have need again for someone to teach you the elementary principles of the actual words of God, and you have come to need milk and not solid food. For everyone who partakes only of milk is unacquainted with the word of righteousness, for he is an infant. But solid food is for the mature, who because of practice have their senses trained to distinguish between good and evil."

This explains why the writer didn't go deeper into the topic of Melchizedek right away. He wanted to address some critical basics before going on to more intricate topics, but their spiritual condition made it hard to explain.

Being "dull of hearing" means the hearer isn't really interested in what God has to say. Not wanting to hear (with the heart) the Word of God points to a genuine spiritual problem and a lack of spiritual maturity. These Christians who felt like giving up with Jesus were also dull of hearing. According to the time they had been followers of Jesus, they should have been much more mature than they were. 

"Milk" corresponds to the elementary principles of verse 12. "Solid food" is the "meatier" material, such as understanding the connection between Jesus and Melchizedek. The idea is that a mature believer should be able to remind themself of the elementary principles of the faith, and then seek to learn the more intricate topics.

These Christians demonstrated immaturity by both their lack of discernment between good and evil and in their contemplation of giving up with Jesus. The mature Christian is marked by their discernment and by their unshakable commitment to Jesus Christ.
